Residence permits · Turkish law

Investor residence permit in Turkey (2026): USD 150,000 for three years, USD 200,000 for five

Dr Alihan Sağır, Turkish lawyer (Avukat), Antalya Bar Association · Prepared: 19 November 2026

1. A new decree was published on 18 November 2026. Presidential Decree No. 11994, Official Gazette issue 33434, sets residence permits for foreigners who invest in Turkey but do not work there. 2. There are two tiers. An investment of at least USD 150,000 (or the equivalent in another currency) gives a three-year permit. At least USD 200,000 gives a five-year permit. 3. Four investments qualify: a bank deposit, government debt instruments, units in a real estate or venture capital investment fund, and contributions to the private pension system. 4. You must hold the investment for half the permit period: 18 months for the three-year permit, 30 months for the five-year permit. 5. Your spouse and your minor or dependent children can be covered under the same provision. 6. It is not a work permit, and it does not lead to citizenship. 7. The detailed rules are not out yet. The Ministry of Interior will set the procedure. At the date of review, nothing had been published.

In short

This guide is for foreigners who want to live in Antalya, Alanya or elsewhere in Turkey without working here, and who are looking at a residence permit by investment. In Antalya the first question is often whether buying a flat is enough. Under this Decree it is not, as explained below.

What was published, and when?

The Decree on Residence Permits to Be Issued to Foreign Investors (Yabancı Yatırımcılara Düzenlenecek İkamet İzinlerine Dair Karar) is Presidential Decree No. 11994. It is dated 17 November 2026 and was published in the Official Gazette on 18 November 2026, issue 33434. It came into force on the day of publication. The Minister of Interior is responsible for carrying it out.

The Decree rests on the Foreigners and International Protection Act (No. 6458), Art. 31(1)(j) and Art. 31(5). Art. 31(1)(j) allows a short-term residence permit (kısa dönem ikamet izni) for people who do not work in Turkey but make an investment of a scope and amount set by the President. The Decree is the instrument that sets them.

The Decree is short. It has six articles and a table. Much of the practical detail is left to the Ministry of Interior (Art. 3), and that is where the open questions below come from.

What are the two tiers?

The table attached to the Decree is simple.

InvestmentResidence permit
At least USD 150,000, or the equivalent in foreign currency3 years
At least USD 200,000, or the equivalent in foreign currency5 years

The amount is stated in US dollars "or the equivalent in foreign currency". So euros, pounds and other currencies can be used. Turkish lira is not mentioned in the Decree.

The Decree does not say which exchange rate counts, for example the rate on the day of investment. That is not yet known.

Which investments qualify?

Article 2 lists four. Each is confirmed by a different authority. The next guide in this series, on the qualifying investments, goes through them in more detail. In brief:

1. Bank deposit. The amount is deposited with a bank operating in Turkey and held for half the permit period. The Banking Regulation and Supervision Agency (BDDK) confirms the deposit.

2. Government debt instruments. The instruments are bought and held for half the permit period. The Ministry of Treasury and Finance confirms the purchase.

3. Fund units. Units (katılma payı) in a real estate investment fund or a venture capital investment fund are bought and held for half the permit period. The Capital Markets Board (SPK) confirms the purchase.

4. Private pension contributions. The amount is paid into the private pension system (bireysel emeklilik sistemi, BES). The person must join a pension plan within the scope set by the Insurance and Private Pension Regulation and Supervision Agency (SEDDK), and stay in the system for half the permit period. SEDDK confirms this.

Buying a flat or house, putting capital into a company and buying shares are not on this list. Property has its own route. See our guide to buying property in Turkey as a foreigner.

Whether two or more of these can be combined to reach the threshold, for example part deposit and part fund, is not clear. The Decree describes each investment separately and does not deal with combining them. This is not yet known.

This article does not say which bank, fund or instrument to choose. That is an investment decision, and the Decree does not rank them.

How long must the money stay invested?

For half the permit period. In figures:

  • three-year permit: 1.5 years (18 months);
  • five-year permit: 2.5 years (30 months).

The Decree does not say what happens after that half-period ends. It does not ask for the investment to be held beyond it. But it also does not say what will be required when you apply to extend the permit. The risk to keep in mind is this: if you withdraw the investment before the holding period is over, the condition for the permit may no longer be met, and under the general rules the permit can then be cancelled (see below). The Decree does not state this in terms, so treat it as a risk and not as a settled rule.

Who does the permit cover?

The permit is for a person who does not work in Turkey and makes the investment. It also covers that person's foreign spouse, and the minor or dependent foreign children of the investor and of the spouse.

The family members receive a short-term residence permit under the same provision, so the Decree covers them directly rather than through a family residence permit (aile ikamet izni).

Is it a work permit?

No. The permit is for people who do not work in Turkey. If you want to work in Turkey, you need a work permit under the International Labour Force Law (No. 6735). That is a separate application. If you plan to run or manage a company in Turkey, see our guide for foreign managers of Turkish companies and our page on company formation in Türkiye.

Does it lead to Turkish citizenship?

No. Article 4 of the Decree says the permits issued under it are not assessed under Art. 12 of the Turkish Citizenship Act (No. 5901). That is the article on exceptional citizenship, which includes citizenship by investment.

The citizenship routes have their own, separate and higher thresholds. For the property route, our existing guide describes a minimum of USD 400,000 and a three-year note on the title deed. The third guide in this series compares the investor permit, the property-based permit and citizenship.

What general conditions apply?

The permit is a short-term residence permit, so the general conditions of Art. 32 of Law No. 6458 apply. In summary, you must:

  • give your reasons for the stay and submit the supporting documents;
  • not be among those banned from entering Turkey (Art. 7);
  • have housing that meets general health and safety standards;
  • provide a criminal record certificate if the authorities ask for one;
  • give your address in Turkey.

Can the permit be cancelled?

Yes, under Art. 33. A permit is refused, cancelled or not extended if the conditions are not met or stop being met, if the permit is used for a purpose other than the one it was given for, or if there is a deportation order or an entry ban. The rules on cancelling a permit because of time spent abroad are to be set by regulation.

If a permit is refused or cancelled, an action can be brought before the administrative court (Art. 31(7)). Hearings are at the court's discretion.

Can the permit be renewed, and what about long-term residence?

Renewal. Short-term permits are normally given for no more than two years at a time. Art. 31(5) makes an exception for investors: permits under 31(1)(j) can be given for periods of up to five years each. The wording of the law leaves the door open to renewal. But what you will need to show at renewal, for example whether the investment must be made again, has not been set. That depends on the Ministry's rules.

Long-term residence. Under Arts. 42 and 43 of Law No. 6458, a long-term residence permit can generally be granted to someone who has stayed in Turkey with a residence permit without interruption for at least eight years, or who meets conditions set by the Ministry. Other conditions apply too: no social assistance in the last three years, sufficient and regular income, valid health insurance, and no threat to public order.

How time on an investor permit will count towards the eight years has not been set out. As a rule the law looks for uninterrupted time on a residence permit. How an investor permit is counted, and how time spent outside Turkey affects it, will have to be assessed in each case.

What is not yet known?

The Decree gives the Ministry of Interior the task of setting "the procedures and principles" for services and other matters (Art. 3). As at 19 November 2026, we found none published. These points are open:

  • where and how to apply: from abroad through a consulate, or in Turkey through the provincial migration office. Not yet known;
  • the documents required, the fees, and any health insurance requirement;
  • which banks and intermediaries are involved, and how the confirmation from BDDK, the Ministry of Treasury and Finance, SPK or SEDDK is obtained;
  • whether instruments can be combined to reach a threshold;
  • which exchange rate is used for the dollar threshold;
  • what is needed to extend the permit after the first period;
  • whether holders of an existing property-based permit can switch to this one. a change of permit type is generally possible in practice, but the Decree says nothing on this point;
  • tax. The tax treatment of deposit interest, fund returns, pension contributions and tax residence is not covered here. Take advice from a tax adviser or accountant.

How can you prepare now?

You cannot apply yet, but some preparation costs nothing and is unlikely to be wasted.

  1. Check your passport. Make sure it is valid for a good period ahead, and that you hold valid passports for any family members who will be included.
  2. Request a criminal record certificate from your home country if you do not have a recent one. The authorities may ask for one (Art. 32).
  3. Think about your address in Turkey. An address is a general condition, and the housing must meet health and safety standards.
  4. Keep your funds traceable. Keep the bank statements that show where the money came from and how it moved to Turkey. The confirming authorities will need to see the investment, and clear records help.
  5. Ask the bank or intermediary in writing how the investment would be confirmed, what the holding period means in practice, and what happens if you need the money earlier. Compare the answers with the Decree itself.
  6. Be careful with promises. Until the Ministry's rules are out, nobody can tell you the procedure with certainty. Be cautious about anyone who gives you a fixed timetable, a fixed price or an assured outcome, or who asks for a payment before the rules exist.
  7. Plan for the holding period. The money has to stay in place for 18 or 30 months as a legal condition of the permit.

Frequently asked questions

How much do I need to invest for a residence permit in Turkey?

At least USD 150,000, or the equivalent in foreign currency, for a three-year permit. At least USD 200,000 for a five-year permit.

Can I buy a flat instead?

Not under this Decree. The four qualifying investments are a bank deposit, government debt instruments, units in a real estate or venture capital investment fund, and pension contributions. Property has a separate residence permit route and a separate citizenship route.

Do I have to keep the money invested?

Yes, for half the permit period: 18 months for the three-year permit and 30 months for the five-year permit. What is required after that, and at renewal, is not yet known.

Can my family come with me?

The Decree covers the investor's foreign spouse and the minor or dependent foreign children of the investor and the spouse. They receive short-term residence permits under the same provision.

Can I work in Turkey with this permit?

No. It is for people who do not work in Turkey. Work needs a separate work permit.

Does it lead to Turkish citizenship?

No. Article 4 of the Decree excludes these permits from the exceptional citizenship rule in Art. 12 of Law No. 5901. Whether time on this permit counts towards the eight years needed for a long-term residence permit has not been set out yet.
