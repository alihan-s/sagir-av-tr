Which investments qualify for Turkey's investor residence permit? Bank deposits, government bonds, fund units and private pensions

Dr Alihan Sağır, Turkish lawyer (Avukat), Antalya Bar Association · Prepared: 19 November 2026

1. The Decree names four instruments: a deposit in a bank operating in Turkey, government debt securities, units in a real estate investment fund or a venture capital investment fund, and contributions to the private pension system (bireysel emeklilik sistemi, BES). 2. The amount must be USD 150,000 or more for a 3-year permit, or USD 200,000 or more for a 5-year permit, in US dollars or the equivalent in another foreign currency. Turkish lira is not listed. 3. Each instrument must be held for half of the permit term: 18 months for a 3-year permit, 30 months for a 5-year permit. 4. A different authority certifies each instrument: BDDK for deposits, the Ministry of Treasury and Finance for government securities, SPK for fund units, SEDDK for pension contributions. 5. Direct property purchase, company capital and shares are not on the list. 6. The implementing rules are not yet published. The Ministry of Interior has not yet set the procedure, so several practical points are open. They are marked below. 7. Withdrawing early may put the permit at risk. The Decree does not say so in terms, but the general cancellation rule in the Foreigners Law could apply.

In short

This is the second article in a series. The first, Turkey's new investor residence permit: what Decree No. 11994 says, covers the permit as a whole, including who can apply and family members. The third, investor residence, property residence and citizenship compared, sets the routes side by side.

For readers in Antalya, Alanya and other parts of Turkey, the question is usually practical: which of the four investments fits your plans for living here. This article sets out what the Decree requires for each of them, without recommending any product.

Which investments does the Decree recognise?

Article 2 of the Decree sets out the instruments. It applies to people who do not work in Turkey and who invest the listed amount, and to their foreign spouse and their minor or dependent foreign children. The permit is a short-term residence permit (kısa dönem ikamet izni) under Art. 31(1)(j) of the Foreigners and International Protection Act (No. 6458). It is not a work permit and it does not lead to citizenship.

The Ministry of Interior is to set the detailed rules (Art. 3 of the Decree). As of 19 November 2026 none have been published.

Do bank deposits qualify, and who certifies them?

Yes. The Decree lists a deposit in banks operating in Turkey, held for half of the permit term. The Banking Regulation and Supervision Agency (BDDK) is the authority that certifies the deposit.

What is settled is the amount (USD 150,000 or USD 200,000 or the foreign currency equivalent), the certifying authority and the holding period. What is not yet settled:

  • the form in which the bank or BDDK confirms the deposit;
  • whether particular account types, such as time deposits or demand accounts, are accepted;
  • which banks are accepted, if the Ministry publishes a list.

The Decree does not say whether the amount can be reduced by interest or exchange rate movements during the holding period. That is also open.

Do government bonds count?

Yes. The Decree refers to government debt securities (devlet borçlanma araçları) bought for the amount above and held for half of the permit term. The Ministry of Treasury and Finance certifies the purchase.

The Decree does not say which securities qualify, whether a particular currency of issue is needed, or how the holding is shown on paper. These points depend on the Ministry's rules. Until they are published, treat any statement about a particular security as unconfirmed.

Can you invest in a real estate or venture capital fund?

Yes. The Decree names units (katılma payı) in a real estate investment fund or a venture capital investment fund, bought and held for half of the permit term. The Capital Markets Board (SPK) certifies the purchase.

The Decree lists these two fund types only. It does not mention other kinds of fund. Open points include which funds are accepted, whether units must be bought through a particular type of intermediary, and how the holding is certified. It also does not say how a change in the unit price during the holding period is treated against the USD 150,000 or USD 200,000 threshold.

Do private pension contributions work?

The Decree allows contributions to the private pension system (BES) as the investment, on two conditions: the person joins pension plans whose scope is set by SEDDK, and stays in the system for half of the permit term. SEDDK certifies this.

The Decree leaves the scope of the plans to SEDDK, so which plans qualify is not known yet. It also does not explain whether contributions have to be made as a lump sum or can be spread, or how the foreign-currency amount is converted. The pension contract's own terms on leaving the system should be read alongside the Decree.

USD or another foreign currency?

The Decree measures the investment in US dollars "or the equivalent in foreign currency". That should cover currencies such as the euro or the pound sterling. Turkish lira is not mentioned as a way to meet the threshold.

Which exchange rate is used, and on what date, is not yet known. The Decree does not say whether the rate on the day of investment, the day of application or some other day is the one that counts.

Can you combine instruments?

The Decree describes each instrument in its own sub-paragraph and does not say whether the threshold can be met by combining them, for example part in a deposit and part in fund units. This is not addressed in the text and is not yet known. The Ministry's rules may settle it. Until then, do not assume a combined investment will be accepted.

What happens if you withdraw early?

The Decree requires each investment to be held for half of the permit term. It does not say what happens if you withdraw before then.

The Foreigners Law has a general rule on cancellation (Art. 33): a permit can be refused, cancelled or not extended when its conditions are not met or no longer exist. Withdrawing the investment before the holding period ends could be treated as a condition ceasing to exist. This is a risk to be aware of, not a rule stated in the Decree. A refusal or cancellation of a short-term permit can be challenged in court (Art. 31(7)).

Two further points are open:

  • The Decree does not require the investment to stay in place after the half-term has passed.
  • Art. 31(5) of the Law allows these permits to be given for up to five years at a time, which suggests renewal is possible. What will be asked at renewal, including whether the investment must be made or held again, has not been set.

What does not qualify?

The following are not in the list in Art. 2 of the Decree:

  • buying a flat, house or land;
  • putting capital into a company;
  • buying shares;
  • an amount in Turkish lira (the Decree refers to US dollars or the equivalent in foreign currency).

Buying property is a separate route. A foreigner who owns property can apply for a short-term residence permit under Art. 31(1)(b) of the Law, on rules the Ministry sets for the type and value of property. See buying property in Turkey as a foreigner and our page on property purchase in Antalya.

If you plan to work in Turkey, run a business or act as a company manager, the investor permit does not cover that. A separate work permit is needed. See foreign managers of a Turkish company and company formation in Türkiye.

The permit also gives no route to citizenship. Under Art. 4 of the Decree it is not considered under Art. 12 of the Turkish Citizenship Act (No. 5901).

Keeping a clean paper trail

The rules on proof are not published, but some habits are sensible whatever they turn out to be:

  • Bring the funds in from abroad and keep the transfer records. Keep the foreign bank statement, the transfer confirmation and the Turkish bank's credit notice together.
  • Keep everything in your own name. Accounts, securities, fund units and pension contracts should be registered to the applicant. If a spouse also needs a permit, check how the Ministry treats joint holdings, as this is not addressed.
  • Keep the confirmations from the bank or intermediary showing the date, the amount, the currency and the instrument.
  • Keep periodic statements covering the holding period, so you can show the investment stayed in place.
  • Do not move the money between instruments or withdraw it without checking the effect on the permit first.

This is about record keeping, not about which product to choose. This article gives no investment advice and does not name any bank, fund or provider. The tax treatment of interest, fund returns and pension contributions, and your tax residence, should be checked with a tax adviser.

Frequently asked questions

Which instruments does Decree No. 11994 accept?

A deposit in a bank operating in Turkey, government debt securities, units in a real estate investment fund or venture capital investment fund, and contributions to the private pension system (BES).

How long do I have to keep the investment?

Half of the permit term: 18 months for a 3-year permit (USD 150,000 or more) and 30 months for a 5-year permit (USD 200,000 or more).

Can I invest in euros or pounds?

The Decree allows US dollars or the equivalent in foreign currency. Turkish lira is not listed. The exchange rate rule is not yet known.

Can I split the amount between a deposit and a fund?

The Decree does not say. It describes each instrument separately and does not address combining them. Wait for the Ministry's rules.

Can I buy a flat or invest in shares instead?

Not under this Decree. Property has its own route under Art. 31(1)(b) of the Foreigners Law, and shares and company capital are not on the list.

What if I take the money out early?

The Decree does not say. The general cancellation rule in Art. 33 of the Foreigners Law could apply if a condition for the permit stops being met, so this is a risk to avoid until the rules are clear.
