Running a foreign-owned company in Türkiye: yearly legal obligations

Dr Alihan Sağır, Turkish lawyer (Avukat), Antalya Bar Association · Prepared: 25 September 2026 · Last content review: 7 November 2026

A company incorporated in Türkiye by a Dutch, Belgian or German parent is a Turkish company in every respect. It keeps Turkish statutory books, holds a general assembly every year, files changes with the trade registry (ticaret sicili) and receives official notices electronically. Because its shareholder is foreign, it also reports to the Ministry of Industry and Technology through an online system called E-TUYS.

In short

Tax returns, payment dates and payroll filings are not covered here. A Turkish certified public accountant (mali müşavir) or tax adviser should handle them. For the choice between a subsidiary, a branch and a liaison office, see Setting up a company in Türkiye as a foreign business.

What must be in place right after incorporation?

  • An accountant. Books are kept in Turkish and in Turkish lira. In practice almost every company appoints a mali müşavir, who also files tax and, usually, social security declarations.
  • Statutory books (Turkish Commercial Code, TTK No. 6102, Art. 64). Both company types keep the accounting books (journal, general ledger, inventory book). A joint-stock company (anonim şirket, A.Ş.) also keeps a share ledger, a board decision book and a general assembly minute book; a limited company (limited şirket, Ltd. Şti.) keeps a share ledger and a general assembly decision book. For A.Ş. and Ltd. companies, the opening certification of the books is done by the trade registry office at registration (in Antalya, the Antalya Trade Registry Directorate). Books kept on paper are also subject to later notarial certifications under Art. 64; books kept electronically are not. For companies incorporated from 1 January 2026, the share ledger and the general assembly minute book are opened in the Electronic Commercial Books System (ETDS) together with registration; the other books follow the relevant accounting and certification rules. Keeping the board decision book electronically is optional. ETDS is separate from the accounting e-book (e-defter) application; whether the company must keep e-books is a question for the accountant.
  • A UETS address and electronic notification. Like all private-law legal entities, joint-stock and limited companies must receive official notices electronically (Notification Law No. 7201, Art. 7/a). These notices run through the National Electronic Notification System (UETS), operated by PTT. A notice is treated as served at the end of the fifth day after it reaches the addressee's electronic address, whether or not anyone has read it, so a court or other official letter sent to an unchecked UETS address can become final. A registered e-mail (KEP) account does not replace the UETS address, and the two should be monitored separately. The tax authority also uses its own electronic notification system, which needs to be watched too. Naming one person to monitor UETS and the other notification channels is a simple precaution.
  • Social security. Once the company employs its first person, it registers as an employer with the Social Security Institution (SGK). The accountant or a payroll provider normally does this. Foreign managers on the payroll need a work permit; see Foreign managers in a Turkish company.
  • Registered address and chamber. The registered address, the chamber membership (for Antalya companies, usually the Antalya Chamber of Commerce and Industry, ATSO) and the yearly dues should be confirmed with the accountant.

The dependency report

A Turkish company controlled by a foreign parent is a dependent company. Within the first three months of the financial year, its management body prepares a report on the company's relations with the controlling company and its affiliates. The report describes the past year's legal transactions with them and the measures taken or avoided in their interest, with performance and counter-performance, and, if there was a loss, whether it was compensated (TTK Art. 199). It is separate from the annual activity report, and neither a general assembly resolution nor an E-TUYS filing replaces it. For a limited company the provisions are applied by analogy. Where a subsidiary has intra-group loans, services or guarantees, a lawyer in Türkiye can advise on the content of the report.

The ordinary general assembly

The ordinary general assembly must be held within three months of the end of each financial year. It approves the financial statements, decides on profit and discharges (ibra) the board or managers. Where the parent is the only shareholder, it holds all the powers of the general assembly and its decisions must be in writing (TTK Art. 408/3 for an A.Ş., Art. 616/3 for a Ltd. Şti.), signed by an authorised representative. It must still be taken every year and entered in the minute book. In an A.Ş., certain assemblies, for example those amending the articles to change the capital or the business purpose, require the attendance of a Ministry of Trade representative. The signatory's authority is normally shown by an extract from the Dutch, Belgian or German commercial register or a power of attorney, apostilled and translated by a sworn translator.

Which changes must be registered, and by when?

Unless a law says otherwise, a registration must be applied for within 15 days of the event, or within one month for persons residing outside the jurisdiction of the trade registry office (TTK Art. 30/1, 30/3), and every change to registered details is itself registered (Art. 31). Typical examples:

  • a new or departing manager or board member, and changes to signing authority;
  • a change of address, company name or business purpose;
  • an increase or decrease of capital;
  • a transfer of shares in a Ltd. Şti. Under TTK Art. 595 it needs a written agreement with notarised signatures and, unless the articles say otherwise, shareholders' approval; the transfer is then registered (Art. 598).

In an A.Ş., the share ledger (pay defteri, Art. 499) must be updated. In both company types the change must also be reflected in E-TUYS.

What if the company starts losing money?

TTK Art. 376 applies to joint-stock companies, and Art. 633 applies it by analogy to limited companies.

  • Half of the total of capital and statutory reserves lost: the board must call the general assembly immediately and propose remedial measures.
  • Two thirds of that total lost: the assembly must decide to continue with one third of the capital or to restore the capital. If it decides neither, the company ends automatically.
  • Over-indebtedness (borca batıklık): if there are signs that debts exceed assets, the board prepares an interim balance sheet. If assets do not cover debts, it notifies the commercial court and requests bankruptcy. The only exception is where, before the bankruptcy decision, creditors holding debts large enough to cover the shortfall and remove the over-indebtedness have agreed in writing to rank behind all other creditors, and court-appointed experts have confirmed that the declaration or agreement is appropriate, genuine and valid (TTK Art. 376/3). A letter from the parent or a creditor alone is not enough; without the sufficient amount and the expert confirmation the duty to notify remains.

For a subsidiary funded by intra-group loans, how the loans are classified can affect the result. A lawyer in Türkiye can review the position before the year-end accounts are approved.

Does the company need a statutory audit?

Some companies are subject to an independent audit because of their activity or status, others once they meet the relevant size thresholds. Companies listed in List I annexed to the Presidential decision can be audited regardless of any size criterion. For the size-based category, under TTK Art. 397/4, the companies that must have an independent audit are determined by a Presidential decision, based on total assets, net sales and number of employees. The thresholds are set by decision and revised from time to time, so the current decision should be checked for each financial year before assuming that a small subsidiary is exempt, and whether the company appears in List I should be checked separately. A company that falls within the audit scope must also maintain a website with certain legally required content (TTK Art. 1524).

What must a foreign-owned company report to the Ministry?

Law No. 4875 removed prior permission for foreign investment. Companies and branches within its scope instead give information to the Ministry of Industry and Technology through E-TUYS (Regulation Art. 5):

  • shareholder and affiliate details, within one month of the user being authorised;
  • an annual activity form (Faaliyet Bilgi Formu), by the end of May each year;
  • an updated shareholder list within one month of a capital increase or decrease or a share transfer;
  • a capital information form within one month of a payment for a capital increase or share transfer.

Access requires an authorised user with a qualified electronic signature, which is worth arranging before the first deadline. Liaison offices (irtibat bürosu) file a separate annual activity report by the end of May.

Frequently asked questions

Is the general assembly required if the parent is the only shareholder?

Yes. The decisions are the parent's, but they must be taken and recorded each year within three months of year-end.

Who is responsible if a deadline is missed?

The managers or board members are responsible for the company's compliance. Some failures carry administrative fines or personal liability.

Does a change in the group's ownership abroad have to be reported in Türkiye?

If the Turkish company's direct shareholders change, through a share transfer or capital change, the shareholder list is updated in E-TUYS within one month. A change higher up the foreign ownership chain does not in every case require a direct E-TUYS update; the direct shareholder notification and the obligations concerning the ultimate beneficial owner should be assessed separately. If the ultimate owners or controlling individuals change, the accountant, and where needed a lawyer in Türkiye, should be told.

Are these duties different in the Antalya Free Zone or an organised industrial zone (OSB)?

The company-law duties are the same. Zone operators may add their own conditions, and the tax treatment differs. See Setting up in Antalya.
