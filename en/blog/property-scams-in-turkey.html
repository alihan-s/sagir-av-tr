Property scams in Turkey (Antalya, Alanya): common schemes and how to protect yourself

Dr Alihan Sağır, Turkish lawyer (Avukat), Antalya Bar Association · Prepared: 19 November 2026

1. Most problems can be checked before you pay. They recur among foreign buyers and come down to a small number of situations. 2. Ownership passes only at the land registry. A receipt for a "reservation" or a deposit paid to an agent transfers nothing. 3. Start with the title deed record (tapu). It shows the owner, mortgages, seizures and other annotations, and whether the flat is registered as completed (kat mülkiyeti) or only as a right to a future flat (kat irtifakı). 4. Check the building too: construction licence, occupancy permit (iskan / yapı kullanma izin belgesi), service-charge debts, property tax and utility bills. 5. The price in the contract should be the real price. An understated amount creates risks for the buyer. 6. A flat does not by itself give residence or citizenship. 7. If something goes wrong, there are several options: a complaint to the public prosecutor, a civil claim and, in some cases, prior mediation. Some claims are subject to time limits, so keep your documents straight away.

In short

What typical schemes look like

Below are eight situations that most often lead to disputes when buying property in Antalya, Alanya and along the whole coast. For each, we describe how it looks in practice and what can be checked in advance.

The seller is not the owner, or acts under a doubtful power of attorney

What it looks like. The seller shows a contract or photographs of documents, but the land register names another person as owner. Or the seller relies on a power of attorney that does not name the property or the powers. Another variant: the flat belongs to spouses, heirs or a company, and only one person signs.

How to check.

  • Request a current title deed record (tapu) and compare the owner with the seller's passport.
  • If the seller acts under a power of attorney, see whether it names the property and the powers, including price and registration. A defective power of attorney is a common cause of delay and disputes.
  • Find out who must sign if the flat belongs to a couple, heirs or a company.

A buyer who relied in good faith on the register is protected by law, while one who knew or should have known of the problem is not. That is why a check before payment matters more than a lawsuit afterwards.

A deposit or "reservation" paid to an agent without transfer at the land registry

What it looks like. You are asked to pay a sum "to reserve" the flat, to sign a form at a sales office or a private agreement, and are promised that registration will follow later.

What to know. A contract to sell real estate is valid only in official form. A promise to sell also requires official form: normally a gayrimenkul satış vaadi sözleşmesi signed before a notary. A form from an agency or sales office does not transfer ownership by itself. Money paid under such a paper is at risk: the seller may change their mind, may have creditors, or may sell the flat to someone else.

How to check and protect yourself.

  • Pay by bank transfer, against registration, with a confirmation that matches the contract.
  • Have a promise to sell made before a notary. It can be annotated on the title deed.
  • Keep every version of the contract, transfer receipts and correspondence.

An invalid paper does not mean the money is lost: a sum paid without a valid legal basis can be claimed back as unjust enrichment. But the claim is time-barred two years after the buyer learns of the right, and in any case ten years after it arises, so waiting is a risk.

The flat is registered only as kat irtifakı: buying off-plan

What it looks like. You are sold a flat in a building under construction or unfinished and promised delivery on time, but the register shows only a right to a future flat.

What to know. Kat irtifakı does not necessarily mean fraud: for an off-plan purchase it is a normal situation. But until there is an occupancy permit it calls for more care: connection of utilities, a mortgage and resale can be difficult. The right to the flat is converted to kat mülkiyeti on the basis of the occupancy permit for the whole building.

What the law provides. Where the buyer is a consumer and the seller a developer, the Consumer Protection Act applies. Among other things:

  • the seller must not contract with consumers before obtaining the construction licence, and must give a pre-contract information form at least one day before the contract;
  • the seller may not ask for any payment before a valid contract exists;
  • the consumer can withdraw within 14 days without giving a reason;
  • the home must be delivered within the period stated in the contract, and in any case within 48 months of the contract date;
  • for projects with thirty or more homes, the seller must, before starting sales, take out building-completion insurance or provide another security set by the regulation.

Whether the Act applies in your case depends on the facts: whether you act as a consumer and the seller as a business. Nationality does not matter. Ask the developer in writing which security exists, and keep the document.

A hidden mortgage, seizure or other annotation on the title deed

What it looks like. The seller assures you the flat is "clean", but an annotation later appears in the title deed record.

How to check. In the current title deed record, look for:

  • a mortgage (ipotek): it must be paid off or taken over;
  • a seizure (haciz) or a court precautionary measure: this usually means a debt to a creditor or a court case;
  • other annotations: a sale promise, a right of pre-emption, a lease, a pending lawsuit.

Once a mortgage or seizure is registered, removing it usually means a dispute with the seller's creditor as well as the seller. The cheaper course is to check the record before paying and to pay against the transfer at the land registry.

Unpaid service charges, taxes and utility bills

What it looks like. After the sale you find that the flat carries debts for service charges to the building management, property tax or utilities.

How to check.

  • Ask the site management for a written statement that no service charges are outstanding. Each owner pays a share of the building's running costs, and late payment carries monthly compensation of 5% (Condominium Act, Art. 20).
  • Check property tax at the municipality.
  • Make sure the electricity, water, gas and internet accounts are settled and can be transferred to your name.

An understated price in the sale contract

What it looks like. You are offered a lower amount in the document than you actually pay, to "save on fees".

Why it is a risk for the buyer. Fees are calculated on the declared price. If it does not reflect the real one, the authorities can charge the difference with a penalty (Fees Act, Art. 63). Money paid above the amount stated in the document is also hard to prove in a later dispute.

Promises of residence or citizenship "with the flat"

What it looks like. In an advertisement or in conversation, the seller says that buying automatically gives the right to live in the country or to obtain a passport.

What the law says. Owning a home gives no automatic right to stay beyond the period allowed for your nationality. A short-term residence permit for property owners is possible, but some neighbourhoods are closed to new registrations. The list changes and should be checked with the provincial migration office (İl Göç İdaresi) before you buy. Citizenship by investment depends on separate conditions: a minimum property value confirmed by a valuation report, and a note on the title deed that the property will not be sold for three years. The conditions are set by regulation and have changed before.

Since 18 November 2026 there has been a separate investor residence permit, but buying a flat does not qualify for it: it is tied to a bank deposit, government bonds, investment fund units or the private pension system. More: Investor residence permit in Turkey.

A building without an occupancy permit, illegal floors and extensions

What it looks like. The building looks finished, but the documents do not match reality: a floor has been added, a balcony enclosed, part of the common area taken into a flat.

How to check.

  • Ask the municipality to confirm that the building has a construction licence and an occupancy permit matching the approved plans, and that there is no demolition order or urban transformation decision.
  • Compare the property itself with the documents. Breaches of the licence and changes made after the permit are common.

Such breaches can lead to demolition orders, which stay with the building whoever owns it. The new owner may bear the cost of bringing the property into line with the documents. Since 2020, municipalities must notify a breach to the land registry, where it is noted in the record (Zoning Act No. 3194, Arts. 32 and 42). Finding the problem before the contract usually costs less than a lawsuit afterwards.

What to do before paying: a checklist

  1. Obtain a current title deed record (tapu) and compare the owner with the seller's documents.
  2. Check for mortgages, seizures, precautionary measures and other annotations.
  3. Find out whether the flat is completed (kat mülkiyeti) or registered as kat irtifakı.
  4. Check with the municipality the construction licence, the occupancy permit, the plans and that there is no demolition order.
  5. Compare the actual flat with the plans: floors, balconies, common areas.
  6. Obtain a written statement that no service charges are outstanding, and check property tax and utility bills.
  7. When buying from a developer, ask in writing which security exists.
  8. Make sure the document states the real price.
  9. Do not pay on a "reservation" or a private form: pay by bank, against registration.
  10. If a power of attorney is needed, agree its text before signing.

The set of checks depends on the particular transaction. A property lawyer in Antalya, or in another city in Turkey, can check these points before you pay.

What to do if you have already been deceived

The right route depends on what exactly happened, who the seller is and what has already been registered.

Keep the evidence. The contract in every version with annexes and plans, bank transfer receipts, messages with the agent and the developer, permits, title deed records, dated photographs and your own timeline: when you paid, when you complained and what the seller answered.

Criminal complaint. Fraud can be reported to the public prosecutor. The criminal complaint and the civil claim are separate procedures.

Civil claims.

  • Return of the money as unjust enrichment, if the contract is invalid: time-barred two years after you learn of the right, and in any case ten years.
  • An action to correct the register (tapu iptal ve tescil), where registration was made without a valid basis, and an action for transfer of ownership by judgment where there is a valid notarised sale promise. These cases are filed at the court where the property is located. The rights of good-faith third parties and any claim for damages are preserved.
  • If the flat was sold twice and the second buyer is already registered, the claim is normally against the seller for a refund and damages. A claim against the second buyer is possible only in limited cases, for example where that buyer knowingly acted together with the seller.
  • Protecting the claim while the case runs: a court can order an interim measure (ihtiyati tedbir) when there is a concern that the right will become much harder or impossible to obtain. If the measure is ordered before the lawsuit is filed, the lawsuit must be filed within two weeks of asking for the measure to be enforced, otherwise the measure lapses.
  • When buying from a developer, the consumer may withdraw from the contract and, for defects, demand a price reduction, free repair or replacement. The conditions and time limits depend on who the seller is.

Which body hears the dispute. It depends on the parties and the claim: the consumer arbitration committee (for disputes below the limit set for 2026, an application is compulsory), the consumer court or the general civil court. For a number of disputes, mediation is a precondition to a lawsuit, for example for money claims in the consumer court and commercial money claims. An action for annulment and registration of the title deed does not require mediation. A lawsuit filed without required mediation is dismissed on procedural grounds, so identify the route before filing.

More on the routes is in Property disputes in Turkey for foreign buyers.

Buying by power of attorney from abroad

You do not have to travel to Turkey to buy or to bring a claim. A power of attorney (vekaletname) can be signed at a Turkish consulate, or before a notary in your own country with an apostille and a sworn Turkish translation. For registration at the land registry, the original or a certified copy with translation is needed. For court cases, the translation must be certified by a Turkish notary.

The power of attorney must name the property and the powers, including price and registration. For disputes, acts such as sending notices, mediation, filing and conducting the case are added. A defective document is a common cause of delay, so it is advisable to agree the text with a lawyer in Turkey before signing. More on the purchase itself: Buying property in Turkey as a foreigner.

Frequently asked questions

How do I check a property in Turkey before buying?

Request a current title deed record (tapu): owner, mortgages, seizures, annotations, type of registration. Then check with the municipality the construction licence and the occupancy permit, and compare the documents with the flat itself. Separately, obtain a statement that no service charges are outstanding and check property tax and utility bills.

Is a "reservation" with an agent binding?

No, not as a sale of real estate: that requires official form. In an off-plan purchase from a developer, however, the consumer is protected: the seller cannot rely on the missing form against the consumer (Consumer Protection Act, Art. 41). The paper remains evidence of the payment and of the terms, and a claim for the return of the money may be possible.

Can I get my money back for a property in Turkey?

Often yes, but the route depends on the contract and the facts: the consumer law with its right to withdraw and the 48-month delivery limit, the general rules on delay, or a claim for unjust enrichment if the form of the contract is invalid.

Do I need mediation before suing?

For money claims, as a rule yes, for example against a developer in the consumer court. An action for annulment and registration of the title deed does not require mediation.

Does buying a flat give residence or citizenship?

Not automatically. A short-term residence permit is possible, but some neighbourhoods are closed to new applications. Citizenship by investment requires separate conditions, including a valuation of the property and a three-year note against sale.

Can I buy a flat or bring a claim without being in Turkey?

Yes, by power of attorney signed at a consulate or before a notary abroad with an apostille and a translation. Receipts and messages can be sent electronically.
