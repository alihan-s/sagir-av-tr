Investor residence, property residence or citizenship by investment in Turkey: how the routes differ

Dr Alihan Sağır, Turkish lawyer (Avukat), Antalya Bar Association · Prepared: 19 November 2026

In short

  1. There are three different routes, with different laws and thresholds. They are not stages of one process.
  2. The new investor permit (Decree No. 11994, Law No. 6458, Art. 31(1)(j)) is for people who do not work in Turkey. It lasts 3 years (from USD 150,000) or 5 years (from USD 200,000), in one of four financial instruments. It covers the spouse and minor or dependent children.
  3. The property-based permit (Art. 31(1)(b)) is a short-term permit of up to 2 years each time. The Ministry sets the type and value of property that qualifies.
  4. Citizenship by investment (Law No. 5901, Art. 12) has its own, higher thresholds. For a home, the figure on this site is USD 400,000 with a three-year note on the title deed.
  5. The investor permit does not count towards citizenship. Art. 4 of the Decree says these permits are not assessed under Art. 12.
  6. None of the residence permits is a work permit.
  7. The implementing rules for the investor permit are not yet published, so several practical points are open.

Many foreigners in Antalya and Alanya hold a residence permit based on property. The new residence permit by investment does not replace that route; it sits beside it, and neither of them is citizenship.

What is the new investor residence permit?

Decree No. 11994 was adopted on 17 November 2026 and published in the Official Gazette on 18 November 2026 (No. 33434). It rests on Art. 31(1)(j) and Art. 31(5) of the Foreigners and International Protection Act (No. 6458). The permit is a short-term residence permit (kısa dönem ikamet izni) for a person who does not work in Turkey but invests at the listed level.

  • Amount and length: at least USD 150,000 (or the equivalent in foreign currency) for 3 years, or at least USD 200,000 for 5 years.
  • Instruments: bank deposits, government debt instruments, real estate investment fund or venture capital investment fund units (katılma payı), and contributions to the private pension system (bireysel emeklilik sistemi, BES). Buying property, company capital and shares are not on the list.
  • Holding period: half of the permit period, so 18 months for a 3-year permit and 30 months for a 5-year permit.
  • Family: the foreign spouse and the minor or dependent foreign children of the investor and the spouse can receive a permit under the same provision.

Our first article, Investor residence permit in Turkey, explains the Decree. The second, Which investments qualify, goes through the four instruments. The Ministry of Interior is to set the procedures and principles, and none had been published on 19 November 2026.

What is the property-based residence permit?

Under Art. 31(1)(b) of Law No. 6458, a foreigner who owns property in Turkey can apply for a short-term residence permit. Short-term permits are normally granted for a maximum of 2 years each time. The type and value of property that qualifies are set by the Ministry (Art. 31(6)), and the Ministry can change these criteria, so check the rules in force when you apply.

Owning a home does not give an automatic right to stay, and the Ministry closes some neighbourhoods to new registrations. Our guide Buying property in Turkey as a foreigner covers the checks before buying and how this permit sits with the purchase. For the purchase itself, see buying property in Antalya as a foreigner: legal checks.

What is citizenship by investment?

Citizenship by investment rests on Art. 12 of the Turkish Citizenship Law (No. 5901), which provides for exceptional acquisition of citizenship. It is a different legal status from a residence permit: it is decided under a different law, and the thresholds are separate and higher.

For the property route, the threshold is property worth at least USD 400,000 (or the equivalent), with a note on the title deed that it will not be sold for three years. Other routes, such as bank deposits, have their own thresholds.

Art. 4 of Decree No. 11994 says that residence permits issued under the Decree are not assessed under Art. 12 of Law No. 5901. In other words, holding an investor permit is not a step towards citizenship. Anyone aiming for citizenship has to meet the citizenship thresholds and apply under that route on its own terms.

Does the investor permit lead to long-term residence?

Possibly, but nothing in the Decree says so. Under Arts. 42-43 of Law No. 6458, a long-term residence permit can be given to a foreigner who has stayed in Turkey with a residence permit for at least eight years without interruption, or who meets conditions set by the Ministry. Further conditions apply: no social assistance in the last three years, sufficient and regular income, valid health insurance and no public order concerns.

The Decree does not say how years on an investor permit will be counted towards the eight years, or how time spent abroad will be treated. Until the Ministry's rules are published, each case has to be assessed on its own facts.

Can you switch from a property permit to an investor permit?

The Decree contains no specific rule on this. A change of permit type is generally possible in Turkish practice, but how a property-permit holder moves to the investor permit has not been regulated. Until the Ministry's rules are out, it is safer not to let one permit lapse before the requirements of the new one are clear.

Can you work in Turkey on these permits?

No. The investor permit is for people who do not work in Turkey, and the property-based permit is not a work permit either. A foreigner who wants to work needs a work permit under the International Labour Force Law (No. 6735). If you intend to run or manage a Turkish company, see foreign managers of Turkish companies, which covers work permits and residence for that case.

Which route fits which situation?

These are points to weigh, not recommendations.

  • If you want to live in Turkey without working, the investor permit is designed for that, within the limits of its four instruments and holding periods. A property purchase is not an investor-permit instrument.
  • If you already own or plan to buy a home, the property-based permit follows from ownership, but the property type and value rules and closed neighbourhoods matter.
  • If your aim is Turkish citizenship, only the citizenship route leads there. The investor permit is not assessed under that route.
  • If you plan to work or run a business, none of the residence permits is enough, and a work permit is needed.
  • If you are weighing the long term, eight years of uninterrupted residence under Arts. 42-43 is a separate question, and how investor-permit years count is not yet known.
  • Taxes on deposit interest, fund returns, pension contributions and tax residence need separate review with a tax adviser.

"Golden visa" is an informal label sometimes used for such programmes. It is not a term of Turkish law, and this article does not use it for any route.

Frequently asked questions

Does the investor residence permit give citizenship?

No. Art. 4 of Decree No. 11994 says the permits issued under it are not assessed under Art. 12 of the Citizenship Law.

Can an investor permit later be turned into citizenship?

Not on the basis of the Decree. Investor permits are not assessed under Art. 12, and citizenship has its own, higher thresholds and its own application.

Is property a qualifying investment for the investor permit?

No. The Decree lists bank deposits, government debt instruments, real estate or venture capital investment fund units, and BES contributions. Property ownership has its own route under Art. 31(1)(b).

Can I work in Turkey with an investor or property residence permit?

No. Neither is a work permit. Work needs a permit under Law No. 6735.

Are the eight years for long-term residence counted from an investor permit?

Not specifically regulated yet. The law asks for eight years' uninterrupted stay with a residence permit, and how investor-permit years and time abroad count is to be assessed case by case.

Can I move from a property permit to the investor permit?

The Decree has no specific rule. The Ministry's implementing rules should be awaited.
