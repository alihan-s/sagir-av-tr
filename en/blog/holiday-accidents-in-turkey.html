Holiday accidents in Turkey: injuries at hotels, on excursions and on the road

Dr Alihan Sağır, Turkish lawyer (Avukat), Antalya Bar Association · Prepared: 19 November 2026

Visitors to Turkey are sometimes injured in a hotel, on an excursion or on the road, and then have to deal with Turkish insurers, operators and authorities from another country. The legal route follows from who was responsible and what the visitor bought: a package holiday, a hotel stay on its own, an excursion, or a hire car. This guide explains the main rules under Turkish law in plain English.

In short

  1. Several parties can be liable. Depending on the facts: the hotel operator, an excursion or boat operator, a travel agency or tour organiser, a driver, the operator of a vehicle and the vehicle's insurer.
  2. Fault-based compensation. Anyone who causes damage by a faulty and unlawful act must make it good (Code of Obligations, Art. 49), and a business is liable for damage caused by its staff in the course of their work unless it proves the required care (Art. 66).
  3. Package holidays have their own rules. A package organiser is liable for any loss from non-performance or defective performance, including where the fault lies with independent service providers (Consumer Protection Act, Art. 51; Package Tour Contracts Regulation, Art. 13).
  4. Road accidents run through compulsory motor insurance. The injured person can claim directly from the insurer, but must first apply to it in writing (Highway Traffic Law, Arts. 91 and 97).
  5. If the vehicle is uninsured or cannot be identified, the Guarantee Account (Güvence Hesabı) can pay for bodily injury (Insurance Law, Art. 14).
  6. Time limits exist. For road accidents, the claim for material damage is time-barred two years after the injured person learns of the damage and who is liable, and in any event ten years after the accident (Art. 109).
  7. Criminal complaints are separate. Negligent injury may be reported to the public prosecutor, and a complaint deadline of six months applies where a complaint is needed.
  8. Evidence is easiest to collect at the time. Reports, photographs, names and medical records are harder to obtain after returning home.

Who can be liable

Turkish law gives the injured person two main legal bases against a business:

  • Contract. A hotel stay, an excursion or a hire car is a contract. If the business does not perform properly, it must make good the resulting loss unless it proves that no fault can be attributed to it (Code of Obligations, Art. 112). The rules on tort apply by analogy to breach of contract (Art. 114).
  • Tort (haksız fiil). Anyone who causes damage by a faulty and unlawful act must make it good (Art. 49). The injured person must in principle prove the damage and the fault (Art. 50).

A business is also answerable for its staff. Under Art. 66, an employer must make good damage caused by an employee in the course of the work given to them, unless it proves that it took the necessary care in choosing, instructing and supervising the employee. In an enterprise, the owner must also make good damage arising from its activities unless it proves that its working organisation was suitable to prevent the damage.

Where the injured person contributed to the damage, the court may reduce the compensation or remove it altogether (Art. 52). This matters in cases such as a fall after drinking, or ignoring safety instructions on an excursion.

Hotel accidents and food poisoning

Slips on wet floors, pool and balcony accidents, and food poisoning are the most common hotel cases. In each, the questions are practical: what exactly happened, whether the hazard was visible or signposted, whether the hotel or its staff had notice of it, and whether the injury is medically linked to the event.

A hotel stay is usually a consumer contract. The provider must perform the service in line with the contract (Consumer Protection Act, Art. 14), and where it is performed defectively the consumer may choose a repeat of the service, a price reduction or withdrawal, and may also claim damages under the Code of Obligations (Art. 15). Compensation for bodily injury is mainly a question of the general rules above.

For food poisoning, a medical report close in time to the illness, test results and the names of other affected guests are central.

Package holidays and excursions

A package tour contract under the Consumer Protection Act is one in which at least two of transport, accommodation and other tourism services are sold together for an all-inclusive price, and the service lasts more than 24 hours or includes an overnight stay (Art. 51). The organiser is liable for any loss the consumer suffers from non-performance or defective performance of the contract, and the consumer may claim appropriate compensation for wasted holiday time (Art. 51(8)).

The Package Tour Contracts Regulation (Paket Tur Sözleşmeleri Yönetmeliği) adds three points:

  • The organiser or agent is liable for any loss from non-performance or defective performance, also where this results from the conduct of independent service providers (Regulation, Art. 13).
  • The organiser is not liable in listed cases, including the participant's own fault, force majeure or an unforeseeable and unpreventable event. Even then, it must help a participant in difficulty quickly (Art. 14).
  • Clauses that limit or remove the organiser's obligations under the Regulation are void (Art. 18).

For an excursion booked separately (a boat trip, quad bike, jeep safari, diving or a paragliding flight), the contract is usually with the excursion company or the agency that sold it. The injured person should find out who sold the trip and who ran it, because both may be answerable, and because the paperwork (ticket, voucher, WhatsApp messages) often names only one of them.

Road accidents

Who is liable

Under the Highway Traffic Law (No. 2918), where the operation of a motor vehicle causes death or injury, the operator of the vehicle (işleten) is liable for the damage. Where the vehicle is run under an enterprise's name or trading title, the operator and the owner of the enterprise are jointly and severally liable (Art. 85). They answer for the driver's fault as for their own. This liability applies, for example, to pedestrians hit by a car, to passengers, and to people injured in a transfer minibus or taxi.

Compulsory motor insurance and the direct claim

Vehicle operators must hold compulsory liability insurance (zorunlu mali sorumluluk sigortası, often called trafik sigortası) (Art. 91). For foreign-plated vehicles without valid Turkish cover, this insurance is arranged at the border (Art. 91). The injured person has a direct right of claim against the insurer, within the limits of the policy. Before suing, the injured person must apply to the insurer in writing. If the insurer does not answer in writing within 15 days, or the answer does not satisfy the claim, the injured person may file a lawsuit or apply to arbitration under the Insurance Law (Art. 97).

Insurers must pay the amounts within the policy limits within eight business days after the claimant has delivered the documents required by the general conditions (Art. 99). Compulsory insurance does not cover some items, including claims for non-pecuniary damages (manevi tazminat), indirect losses such as loss of profit, and the part of the loss matching the claimant's own fault (Art. 92). A claim for non-pecuniary damages for bodily injury may be made separately against the person liable (Code of Obligations, Art. 56).

Insurance Arbitration Commission

If the insurer refuses or does not answer, a dispute with the insurer can be taken to the Insurance Arbitration Commission (Sigorta Tahkim Komisyonu), set up under the Insurance Law (No. 5684, Art. 30). To apply, the person must first have applied to the insurer and have documented a full or partial refusal. The insurer's failure to answer in writing within 15 business days is also enough (Art. 30(13)). The Commission cannot be used for a dispute that is already before a court or the consumer arbitration committee (Art. 30(14)). The arbitrators decide on the documents and must give a decision within four months of their appointment (Art. 30(15) and (16)).

Uninsured or unidentified vehicles

The Guarantee Account (Güvence Hesabı), held at the Insurance Association of Turkey, pays for bodily injury where the insured cannot be identified (for example a hit-and-run) and where the vehicle's operator had no valid insurance, within the cover limits valid on the date of the accident (Insurance Law, Art. 14). Disputes with the Account over payments can also go to the Insurance Arbitration Commission (Art. 30(1)). The Account's website describes an online damage application.

Hire cars

If you were driving a hire car, fault, the rental contract and the attached insurance decide the position. Keep the rental agreement, the insurance certificate and any accident report form.

Time limit

Claims for material damage from motor vehicle accidents are time-barred two years after the injured person learns of the damage and who is liable, and in any event ten years after the date of the accident (Highway Traffic Law, Art. 109). Where the act is also a crime for which the Criminal Code provides a longer limitation period, that longer period applies. A step that interrupts the limitation period against the person liable also interrupts it against the insurer, and the other way round. For tort claims outside road traffic, Art. 72 of the Code of Obligations uses the same two-year and ten-year structure.

Lawsuits over motor accident liability may be filed where the insurer's branch or agent is located, or where the accident happened (Art. 110).

Police reports and criminal complaints

After a road accident, the police or the gendarmerie normally prepare an accident report (kaza tespit tutanağı), and the report can matter later for fault. A criminal investigation can follow where an accident causes injury or death by negligence. Negligent injury is an offence under Art. 89 of the Turkish Criminal Code, and negligent death under Art. 85.

For negligent injury, the investigation and prosecution generally depend on a complaint by the injured person, with limited exceptions (Art. 89(5)). Where a complaint is required, it must be made within six months from the day the person learned of the act and of who committed it (Art. 73). A complaint is filed with the public prosecutor, and a lawyer can file it for someone who has returned abroad. The mechanics of the criminal route are explained in Medical malpractice in Turkey: criminal complaints, Ministry of Health complaints and claims against public hospitals. The criminal case and the compensation claim are separate procedures.

What to keep before flying home

  • Medical reports, discharge papers, X-rays and receipts from every hospital or clinic.
  • Photographs of the scene, the vehicle plates, the hazard (wet floor, missing railing) and the injury, with dates.
  • Names and contact details of witnesses, the driver and the staff involved, with the plate number and insurer of any vehicle.
  • The police, gendarmerie or hotel incident report, or its reference number.
  • Booking confirmations, vouchers, tickets, the rental agreement and travel insurance documents.
  • Messages with the hotel, agency or operator.

Holidays sold in the UK and travel insurance

A holiday sold by a UK tour operator may give rise to a separate claim in the UK under UK package travel rules. UK law is outside the scope of this guide. Travel insurance may also cover medical costs, repatriation and legal expenses, so the policy terms and its notice requirements are worth reading early.

For treatment received in Turkey that went wrong, rather than an accident, see Medical or dental treatment gone wrong in Turkey: legal options for UK patients.

A claim from the UK can usually be handled with a power of attorney given to a lawyer in Turkey.

Frequently asked questions

Can I claim compensation in Turkey if I slipped at a hotel?

Possibly. A hotel can be liable under its contract (Code of Obligations, Art. 112) and in tort, including for its staff's acts (Arts. 49 and 66). The cause of the fall, what the hotel knew or should have known, and your own contribution decide the outcome, and your contribution can reduce compensation (Art. 52).

Who is responsible if I am hurt on a boat trip or quad bike excursion in Turkey?

The company operating the excursion can be liable under contract and tort. If the trip was part of a package, the organiser is liable also for independent service providers (Regulation, Art. 13).

Can I claim against the insurer after a road accident in Turkey?

Yes, within the limits of the compulsory motor insurance. You have a direct claim against the insurer, but must first apply in writing. If it does not answer within 15 days or does not satisfy the claim, you can sue or go to the Insurance Arbitration Commission (Highway Traffic Law, Art. 97).

What if the driver who hit me has no insurance or drove off?

The Guarantee Account can pay for bodily injury where the vehicle is uninsured or cannot be identified, within the cover limits valid on the date of the accident (Insurance Law, Art. 14).

How long do I have to bring a claim after a road accident in Turkey?

For material damage, two years from when you learn of the damage and who is liable, and in any event ten years from the accident (Highway Traffic Law, Art. 109). Separate deadlines apply to criminal complaints, where a six-month period can apply (Criminal Code, Art. 73).

Can I make a claim from the UK after I go home?

Often yes. A lawyer in Turkey can act under a power of attorney, and insurers, the Insurance Arbitration Commission and courts deal with written submissions. Medical records, photographs and reports collected at the time are usually the core evidence.
