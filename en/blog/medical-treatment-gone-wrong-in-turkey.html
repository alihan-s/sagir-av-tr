Medical or dental treatment gone wrong in Turkey: legal options for UK patients

Dr Alihan Sağır, Turkish lawyer (Avukat), Antalya Bar Association · Prepared: 19 November 2026

A British patient who has had dental work (including "Turkey teeth" veneers and crowns), cosmetic surgery or other treatment in Turkey and is unhappy with the result faces a legal map with more than one route. Which route fits depends mainly on whether the provider was private or public, and on what the patient wants: a refund, the cost of corrective treatment, compensation, or a criminal investigation. This guide gives the overview. Two companion guides cover the details of each track.

In short

  1. Private or public provider? This decides the target of any claim. A private clinic, dentist or doctor is sued under private law. A public hospital is not sued directly through the doctor: the claim goes against the administration.
  2. There are four tracks. (a) A civil claim for a refund and compensation. (b) A criminal complaint, for example for negligent injury. (c) A complaint to the Ministry of Health and other bodies. (d) For public hospitals, a claim against the State in the administrative courts. They can run side by side.
  3. The agency or facilitator matters. In health tourism, both the facility and the intermediary company need an authorisation certificate under the 2025 regulation, and the regulation makes both responsible for the services they provide.
  4. Evidence comes first. Medical records, photographs, X-rays, invoices, payment proof and all messages should be gathered and kept, and a second opinion report is usually needed.
  5. Time limits differ by track. A criminal complaint for most forms of negligent injury must be made within six months of learning of the act and the person responsible. Other limits are longer but not unlimited.
  6. You do not have to be in Turkey. A power of attorney lets a lawyer in Turkey act for you.
  7. UK law may also be relevant. A claim in the UK falls under UK law, which this guide does not cover.

Private or public provider: why it matters

Most dental, cosmetic, hair transplant and similar procedures sold to visitors are carried out in private clinics and hospitals. The patient's relationship is then a private-law relationship with the clinic and, in many cases, with the doctor or dentist as well. Claims are made against them, and a civil court decides.

Some patients are treated in State hospitals or university hospitals, for example after an emergency. Here the Constitution (Art. 129, paragraph 5) provides that compensation claims arising from the faults of civil servants and other public officials, committed while exercising their powers, can be brought only against the administration, with the administration keeping a right of recourse against the official. The treating doctor is therefore not the defendant in a civil claim. The route is the administrative courts, described below.

The four tracks

1. Civil claim against a private provider

The patient may claim a refund of the price, the cost of corrective treatment and compensation for material and non-material loss (maddi ve manevi tazminat) from the clinic, the doctor or dentist and sometimes the intermediary. If the dispute is a consumer dispute, mediation before suing is mandatory under the Consumer Protection Law (Law No. 6502, Art. 73/A), with exceptions for disputes within the jurisdiction of the consumer arbitration committee (tüketici hakem heyeti). In 2026 that committee handles disputes below 186,000 TL. The detail, including the legal basis of the claim and the evidence needed, is in Claims against a private clinic or dentist in Turkey.

2. Criminal complaint

Causing injury through negligence is a crime under the Turkish Criminal Code (Law No. 5237, Art. 89), and causing death through negligence is a crime under Art. 85. For negligent injury, the investigation and prosecution depend on a complaint by the injured person (Art. 89(5)), except where the act was committed with conscious negligence (bilinçli taksir) and is not the basic form of Art. 89(1). The complaint is made to the public prosecutor's office or to the police or gendarmerie, in writing or orally on record (Code of Criminal Procedure, Art. 158). A criminal case and a civil claim are separate and may exist at the same time. The mechanics are in Medical malpractice in Turkey: criminal complaints, Ministry of Health complaints and claims against public hospitals.

3. Complaints to the Ministry of Health and other bodies

A complaint can be sent to the patient rights unit (hasta hakları birimi) of a public hospital, to the Ministry of Health's communication centre SABİM (Alo 184), to the Presidential Communication Centre CİMER, or to the Provincial Health Directorate (İl Sağlık Müdürlüğü). Under the Patient Rights Regulation, these rights apply to patients of public and private institutions. A complaint is an administrative tool. It can lead to an inspection of the facility or the intermediary, but it does not by itself produce compensation. For facilities and intermediaries within the health tourism system, the regulation also requires the provider to assess every complaint and take corrective and preventive action (Art. 6(4)).

4. Claim against the State for treatment in a public hospital

Where the harm arose in a public hospital, the claim is a full remedy action (tam yargı davası) in the administrative court. The law requires a prior written application to the administration within one year of learning of the act and in any case within five years of the act (Administrative Procedure Law No. 2577, Art. 13). If the administration refuses, or does not answer within 30 days, the lawsuit can be filed within 60 days at the administrative court (Arts. 7 and 13). Details are in the criminal and administrative guide linked above.

The agency or facilitator

Many UK patients book through an agency or "facilitator". Turkey regulates this in the International Health Tourism and Tourist Health Regulation (Official Gazette, 26 April 2025, No. 32882), which replaced the 2017 regulation. Under it:

  • Health facilities and intermediary companies both need an international health tourism authorisation certificate (yetki belgesi). The Ministry of Health issues it to facilities, and USHAŞ, a company designated by the Ministry, issues it to intermediaries (Art. 5).
  • A facility may not take intermediary services from persons or companies without a certificate (Art. 6(1)(e)).
  • The facility and the intermediary are responsible for the health and intermediary services given to the international patient, depending on the type of service, and the regulation states that medical malpractice falls within this responsibility (Art. 6(3)). The words "complications and" in the same provision were stayed by the Council of State (10th Chamber, 25 December 2025, E.2025/3198).
  • Surgical and interventional procedures in an operating theatre require the facility to hold complication insurance (Art. 6(1)(c)).
  • On request, the facility must give an itemised invoice, laboratory and radiology results and prescriptions free of charge (Art. 12(1)).

Whether the agency you dealt with was authorised can be one of the first questions in a claim. The contract, the invoice and the agency's messages usually show who promised what.

First steps and evidence

These are practical points and do not depend on which track is chosen.

  • Request the complete medical file: treatment notes, consent forms, X-rays and scans, laboratory results, prescriptions, the materials used (brand and batch information for implants or crowns). The facility has to provide laboratory and radiology results and prescriptions on request in health tourism cases.
  • Take dated photographs of the problem and keep taking them as it changes.
  • Keep the contract, quotations, invoices and proof of every payment, and all messages with the clinic and the agency, including WhatsApp and email.
  • Obtain a second opinion from an independent doctor or dentist, ideally in writing and describing the condition, the likely cause and what corrective treatment would be needed. Do not let corrective work destroy the evidence before it has been recorded, and keep the receipts for any further treatment.
  • Write down the dates: the treatment, when the problem was noticed and when you learned it was linked to the treatment. Several time limits start from those dates.

Time limits in outline

Each track has its own time limit. In outline:

  • Criminal complaint: where a complaint is required, it must be made within six months of the day on which the person entitled to complain learned of the act and of who committed it (Criminal Code, Art. 73).
  • Claim against the State: the prior application within one year of learning of the act and five years from the act, then the lawsuit within 60 days of refusal (Law No. 2577, Arts. 7 and 13).
  • Civil claim against a private provider: a damages claim in tort expires two years after the injured person learns of the damage and the person liable, and in any event ten years after the act, unless the criminal law provides a longer limitation period for the act (Code of Obligations, Art. 72). Other claims fall under the general ten-year period (Art. 146).

The safest working assumption is that the shortest period is the one that governs the first step.

Acting from the UK: power of attorney

None of the tracks requires the patient to live in Turkey. A power of attorney signed at a Turkish consulate in the UK, or signed before a UK notary public and then apostilled, translated into Turkish by a sworn translator and certified by a Turkish notary, allows a lawyer in Turkey to act: to request the medical file, file the complaint, attend mediation and represent the patient before the court. See Power of attorney for Turkey from the UK. A court expert (bilirkişi) or the Forensic Medicine Institution (Adli Tıp Kurumu) may need to examine the patient, and this may require a visit. That is a matter for the particular case.

What UK law may offer

The UK is a separate question. A UK court may or may not have jurisdiction over a Turkish clinic, an agency based in the UK may be subject to UK consumer rules, and a UK judgment may or may not be enforceable in Turkey, and the reverse. Travel or medical insurance and a card payment may also matter. These are questions for a UK solicitor.

For related practical situations, see Holiday accidents in Turkey for injuries that are not medical treatment.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sue a dentist in Turkey from the UK?

A civil claim against a private dentist in Turkey is normally brought in Turkey, and it can be run from the UK by power of attorney. A claim before a UK court is governed by UK law.

Can I report a Turkish clinic or dentist to the authorities?

Yes. A criminal complaint can be made to the public prosecutor, and a complaint about a facility can be made to the Ministry of Health through SABİM, CİMER or the Provincial Health Directorate. A complaint does not itself result in compensation.

Is the agency or facilitator who booked my treatment responsible?

It can be. Under the 2025 health tourism regulation, intermediary companies need their own authorisation certificate and are responsible for the intermediary services they provide, and the facility and the intermediary are both responsible for services including medical malpractice after treatment. An agency that sold the treatment, took the payment or promised the result can be liable alongside the clinic.

What if I was treated in a public hospital?

A compensation claim lies against the administration, not against the doctor personally (Constitution, Art. 129). A written application to the administration comes first, followed by a lawsuit in the administrative court if the application is refused or unanswered.

How long do I have to take action?

Each track has its own period. A criminal complaint for negligent injury generally must be made within six months of learning of the act and the person responsible. A prior application against a public hospital must be made within one year of learning of the act. A civil damages claim in tort expires two years after learning of the damage and the person liable.

What evidence should I collect before going home?

The full medical file, X-rays and scans, photographs, the contract and invoices, proof of payment and all messages with the clinic and the agency. A second opinion from an independent doctor or dentist is also usually needed.

Do I have to travel to Turkey to bring a claim?

Not for the main steps. A power of attorney lets a lawyer in Turkey act. A medical examination ordered by a court or the Forensic Medicine Institution may require the patient to attend in some cases.
