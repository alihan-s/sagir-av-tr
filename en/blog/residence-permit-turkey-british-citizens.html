Residence permit in Turkey for British citizens: types, documents and how the application works

Dr Alihan Sağır, Turkish lawyer (Avukat), Antalya Bar Association · Prepared: 19 November 2026

In short

  1. Visa-free entry is limited to 90 days in any 180. UK travel advice says holders of a full British citizen passport can visit Turkey for tourism or business within that limit. Anyone staying longer needs a residence permit (Law No. 6458, Art. 19).
  2. There is no permit that comes with a British passport. Since Brexit, British citizens are third-country nationals and have no EU-related advantage in Turkey.
  3. For most British residents, the relevant permit is a short-term permit. The usual bases are owning property (Art. 31(1)(b)), tourism purposes (Art. 31(1)(e)) or, for relatives, a family residence permit (Arts. 34-35).
  4. The application starts online at e-ikamet.goc.gov.tr and is completed at an appointment with the Provincial Directorate of Migration Management. Fees are set every year.
  5. Long-term residence is a separate status, open after eight years of uninterrupted residence under a permit (Arts. 42-43).
  6. A residence permit is not a work permit, and it does not lead to citizenship by itself.

Many of our British readers live in or near Kaş and Kalkan, or in Alanya and Antalya. The rules are national, and applications go to the provincial migration office for the province where you live.

Can I live in Turkey as a British citizen?

Yes, but not on visa-free status alone. UK government travel advice says holders of a full British citizen passport can visit Turkey without a visa for up to 90 days in any 180-day period, for tourism or business. Under Art. 19 of Law No. 6458, a foreigner who will stay longer than the period allowed by a visa or visa exemption must obtain a residence permit. Art. 20 exempts people who stay up to 90 days under a visa or exemption, for that period.

The 180-day window is rolling, so leaving and re-entering does not reset the count. Exceeding a visa or exemption period by more than ten days can lead to a removal decision (Art. 54(1)(e)).

Which residence permits are relevant to British citizens?

The Act gives several types. These are the ones we most often see in Antalya, Kaş, Kalkan and Alanya.

Short-term permit for property owners (Art. 31(1)(b)). A foreigner who owns property in Turkey can apply. Short-term permits are normally granted for up to 2 years each time (Art. 31(2)). The type and value of property that qualifies is set by the Ministry (Art. 31(6)), so the rules in force on the day of application matter.

Short-term permit for tourism purposes (Art. 31(1)(e)). Often used by people who rent rather than own.

Family residence permit (Arts. 34-35). A foreign spouse and minor or dependent children of a person who holds a residence permit can receive a family permit of up to three years at a time, though never longer than the sponsor's own permit. The sponsor must meet conditions on income, housing, health insurance for the whole family and a clean record on family-order offences, and generally has held a residence permit for at least a year (Art. 35).

Investor permit. Decree No. 11994 (18 November 2026) created a permit for people who do not work in Turkey but invest in four financial instruments. Property is not one of them, and the implementing rules were not yet published on 19 November 2026. See Investor residence permit in Turkey and Investor residence, property residence or citizenship.

Student permit. For people enrolled in education in Turkey, with its own document list on the e-ikamet site. Other short-term grounds include research, medical treatment and Turkish language courses (Art. 31(1)).

I own a villa in Kalkan. Can I get a residence permit?

Ownership is a ground for a short-term permit under Art. 31(1)(b), but it is not automatic. The property has to meet the Ministry's criteria for type and value, the general conditions in Art. 32 apply, and the Ministry can close some neighbourhoods to new registrations. A villa in Kalkan or elsewhere in Kaş district is dealt with in the same way as a flat in Alanya: the application goes through the provincial directorate for the province where the property is, which for Kaş and Kalkan is Antalya.

You will need the title deed (tapu) and an address document: the e-ikamet site lists a copy of the title deed with the building number document (numarataj belgesi) for someone staying in their own home. Check that the property is registered in your name and the address is in the address registration system before you book. For checks before buying, see Buying property in Turkey as a foreigner and Property scams in Turkey. For the purchase itself, see buying property in Antalya as a foreigner: legal checks.

How do I apply for a residence permit?

The Act says applications are made at consulates abroad (Art. 21), with exceptions that allow an application from within Turkey (Art. 22). In practice, most applications by foreigners already in Turkey go through the online system. The usual steps are:

  1. Pre-application on e-ikamet.goc.gov.tr. The official site says appointments are made free of charge on that site.
  2. Appointment at the Provincial Directorate of Migration Management for the province where you live or will live.
  3. Payment of the fee and the card charge.
  4. Evaluation and card. The directorate can ask for more documents before approving.

A permit that is not used within six months of being granted loses its validity (Art. 19).

What documents do I need?

The e-ikamet site publishes a list for each permit type, and the list can change. The common documents listed there include:

  • the application form, signed by the applicant, a legal representative or a lawyer with a power of attorney;
  • a copy of the passport page with identity details and photograph (bring the original on the appointment day);
  • the receipt showing that the permit card charge has been paid;
  • a document showing where you will stay, such as a title deed with a building number document, a notarised rental contract, or a hotel booking with proof of payment;
  • for extensions and changes, an up-to-date residence certificate (yerleşim yeri belgesi) from e-Devlet and a recent utility bill or subscription contract.

Health insurance is part of the process, and the e-ikamet site says that for student permits private or general insurance is verified through the system. Check the list for your own permit type. Proof of sufficient funds may also be requested, and the directorate can ask for a criminal record certificate from your country (Art. 32). Your passport must be valid for at least 60 days longer than the permit you ask for (Art. 21(2)).

Foreign civil documents, such as marriage or birth certificates for a family permit, normally need an apostille and a notarised Turkish translation.

How long does it take, and what does it cost?

Time. We cannot give a reliable figure. It depends on the permit type, the province, the completeness of the file and the load at the directorate.

Fees. The residence permit fee (harç) and the card charge are set every year, and the fee depends on permit type and length. We do not give figures here because we could not confirm the 2026 tariff from an official source for this article. Check the amounts in the e-ikamet system when you apply, and the Revenue Administration (gib.gov.tr) tariff. The fee is paid online or at tax offices and agreed banks.

Extensions. Apply from 60 days before the permit ends, and in any case before it ends (Art. 24).

Are there new rules for short-term permits in 2026?

The main change in 2026 is the investor permit under Decree No. 11994, published on 18 November 2026. It is a new kind of short-term permit for people who do not work in Turkey and invest at least USD 150,000 (3 years) or USD 200,000 (5 years) in bank deposits, government debt instruments, fund units or private pension contributions. Its implementing rules were not yet published on 19 November 2026.

For property-based and tourism permits, we have not found a 2026 amendment to the Act. Keep in mind that the Ministry can change the property criteria (Art. 31(6)) and the list of closed neighbourhoods without amending the Act, so the position on the day you apply is what counts.

How can I get permanent residency in Turkey?

Turkey does not have "permanent residence" by that name for most foreigners. The nearest status is the long-term residence permit (uzun dönem ikamet izni). Under Arts. 42-43 of Law No. 6458, it can be given to a foreigner who has stayed in Turkey with a residence permit for at least eight years without interruption, or who meets conditions set by the Ministry. Further conditions: no social assistance in the last three years, sufficient and regular income, valid health insurance and no threat to public order or security.

Long-term residence is a status in its own right. It is not citizenship, and it is not a work permit. If you plan to work or run a Turkish company, you need a work permit under the International Labour Force Law (No. 6735). See foreign managers of Turkish companies.

What if my application is refused?

A refusal or cancellation of a short-term permit can be challenged in the administrative court (Art. 31(7) refers to court applications against refusals and cancellations). Time limits for filing apply, and we have not confirmed the exact period here, so ask a lawyer promptly after you receive a notice. Common reasons for refusal include missing documents, unsuitable accommodation, a conflict with Art. 7 on entry bans, or a property that does not meet the Ministry's criteria.

Frequently asked questions

Do British citizens need a visa for Turkey?

Not for a visit of up to 90 days in any 180-day period, according to UK government travel advice. Longer stays need a residence permit.

Can I get a residence permit because I own property in Kalkan or Kaş?

Property ownership is a ground for a short-term permit under Art. 31(1)(b), subject to the Ministry's criteria on the type and value of the property and to closed neighbourhoods. Applications are made to the Antalya provincial directorate.

Can I apply for permanent residence?

The long-term residence permit needs eight years of uninterrupted residence under a permit, plus income, insurance and other conditions.
