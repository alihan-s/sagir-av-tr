Platform agreements and venture company obligations in equity- and debt-based crowdfunding under Turkish law

Av. Dr. Alihan Sağır · Prepared: 31 October 2026

Crowdfunding enables technology and production ventures in Türkiye to raise funds from the public through the capital markets, operating at the intersection of public-law securities regulation and private contract law. Rather than a single bilateral arrangement, a crowdfunding campaign establishes a triangular structure of linked contracts connecting the licensed platform, the entrepreneur or venture company, and investors.

Capital markets exemptions and the scope of general private law

Crowdfunding platforms are electronic intermediaries licensed by the Capital Markets Board (CMB) to mediate equity-based or debt-based fundraising from the public (Capital Markets Law No. 6362 [CML], Art. 35/A(1)–(2); CMB Communiqué on Crowdfunding No. III-35/A.2). The legislature exempted crowdfunding platforms and transactions from investment-services licensing and stock-exchange or market-operator rules (CML Art. 35/A(4)), excluded debt-based crowdfunding from banking legislation (Art. 35/A(1)), and carved crowdfunding out of classical public-offering, prospectus, issuer/public-company status, the 500-shareholder public-company threshold, and investor-compensation regimes (CML Arts. 3(1)(e), 3(1)(h), 3(1)(z), 4(1) and 16(1)). Conversely, the private-law relationships comprising the platform (campaign) agreement between the platform and the entrepreneur or venture company, the membership agreement between the platform and investors, and the shareholding or debt-instrument relationship between the funded company and investors remain governed by general provisions of private law (CML Art. 35/A(5)).

Parties to the platform agreement, core obligations and campaign mechanics

Under Communiqué No. III-35/A.2 (Arts. 4(1), 16 and 19), an equity-based crowdfunding platform agreement may be executed either by an existing venture company or by natural-person entrepreneurs prior to incorporation; if the campaign succeeds, a joint-stock funded company (anonim şirket) must be incorporated—or an existing limited liability company converted into a joint-stock company—before collected funds are released. In debt-based crowdfunding, because funds may be raised only through the sale of debt instruments, only an incorporated venture company may apply. The platform agreement regulates preliminary review and submission to the investment committee, operation of the campaign page, escrow blocking of funds with the custody agent, transfer of proceeds upon reaching the target or refund to investors otherwise, and platform fees. Exclusivity covenants, trade-secret protection and post-campaign reporting obligations require close review prior to signature.

Investment committee approval of the information form and joint and several liability

Investor disclosure in Turkish crowdfunding rests not on a prospectus, but on an information form prepared under CMB standards and approved by the platform’s investment committee prior to publication on the campaign page (Communiqué III-35/A.2, Arts. 9, 16–17 and 19–20). Natural persons and legal entities signing the information form—encompassing both the entrepreneur or venture company signatories and the investment committee members approving the form—are jointly and severally liable for damages arising from inaccurate, misleading or incomplete information therein (CML Art. 35/A(6)). Disclosures regarding the technical stage of the product or project, material contracts, financial statements, planned use of funds and risk factors must be grounded in verifiable documentation.

Joint-stock share structure, pre-emptive rights and post-campaign audit of proceeds

Because equity-based crowdfunding proceeds may be transferred only against newly issued shares in a capital increase, allocating those shares to campaign investors requires restricting or abolishing existing shareholders’ statutory pre-emptive rights by general assembly resolution (or non-exercise thereof) under Article 461 of the Turkish Commercial Code No. 6102 (TCC), defining share classes, and reconciling right-of-first-refusal and anti-dilution protection clauses contained in prior shareholders’ agreements. For funded companies whose shares are monitored in dematerialised form at the Central Securities Depository (MKK), only Articles 29, 30(2) and 30(5) CML (governing general assembly call/agenda rules, MKK shareholder lists and the electronic general assembly system) apply by analogy within principles set by the CMB (CML Art. 35/A(2); Communiqué III-35/A.2, Art. 25). Following campaign completion, proper application of proceeds in accordance with the information form remains subject to fund-utilisation reporting, special-purpose independent audit oversight (Communiqué III-35/A.2, Arts. 21–22) and ongoing disclosure duties.
