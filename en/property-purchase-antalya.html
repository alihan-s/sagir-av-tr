Buying property in Antalya as a foreigner: legal checks before you pay

Sağır Hukuk Bürosu assists foreign buyers purchasing property and real estate across Antalya province, including Kaş, Kalkan, Alanya, Manavgat, Side and Kemer. We review title deed records, building and permit documents, and contracts before a purchase, and assist with the land registry transfer. You can write in English or Turkish by WhatsApp or the contact form. Founded in 1988, our office is in Muratpaşa, Antalya city.

Before you pay or sign

We review the title deed record, the building and its permits, and the proposed sale contract before a purchase. The checks depend on whether the property is completed, under construction or being bought from a private owner.

  • Title deed and seller. The registered owner, signing authority, mortgages, seizures and other annotations.
  • Building and permits. The status of the flat, construction and occupancy permits, and information on outstanding property tax and building charges.
  • Contract and payment. The terms of a reservation, deposit or sale agreement, what is being promised and how payment relates to the transfer.
  • Off-plan purchases. The developer, the project documents and the contract for a home that is not yet completed.

The legal rules and limits are explained in Buying property in Turkey as a foreigner.

Who acts for the buyer

In many purchases the estate agent or the developer's sales office prepares the paperwork and arranges the appointment at the land registry. Their role is to complete the sale. That is a normal part of the market, but they are not acting for you.

A lawyer you instruct yourself acts only for you. The work is to read the title deed record, the municipality file and the contract from the buyer's side, and to tell you in English what they say and what is missing.

This matters most before you pay. Many buyers sign a reservation form or a private sales agreement first, often drawn up by the agency or the developer's sales office. Such a paper may be formally invalid, because a contract to sell real estate is valid only in official form. A reservation deposit is not a transfer. If the seller changes their mind, has creditors or sells to someone else, the money is not necessarily lost, but getting it back can mean a claim against the seller. Ask who the lawyer or adviser you are dealing with is acting for, and instruct your own where you want advice on your side.

Common problems in purchases in Antalya, Kaş, Kalkan and Alanya

Most problems foreign buyers face come from a small number of situations. Each can be checked before you pay.

  • Burdens on the title deed. A mortgage, a seizure, a precautionary injunction or an annotation such as a pending lawsuit can sit on the record.
  • Unfinished building or no occupancy permit. A flat registered only as kat irtifakı (floor easement, registered before the building is completed) needs more care. Utilities, mortgages and resale can be affected until the occupancy permit (iskan) exists.
  • Permits that do not match the building. Extra floors and enclosed balconies that differ from the approved plans are common. A demolition order or an urban-transformation decision can also affect the building.
  • Debts attached to the flat. Unpaid service charges, property tax and utility accounts should be cleared before transfer.
  • Off-plan delays. Where the buyer is a consumer, the Consumer Protection Act sets rules for pre-paid housing sales, including a delivery limit of 48 months.
  • A seller without authority. Check who must sign where the property belongs to a couple, heirs or a company.
  • A power of attorney that is too narrow or defective. The document should name the property and the powers given, including the price and registration. A defective document is a common reason for delay.
  • Payment before the transfer. Payment should be by bank and against registration, with proof that matches the contract. The deed should also show the real price.

If a purchase has already gone wrong, see Property disputes in Turkey for foreign buyers.

The transfer and buying from abroad

We assist with preparing the transaction and attending the land registry transfer. A foreign buyer is generally required to sell the purchase price in foreign currency to a bank and give the registry the receipt (döviz alım belgesi). If you intend to buy through a power of attorney, the wording and documents should be checked before you sign abroad. Translation and notarial requirements depend on the documents and the transaction.

Contact us before paying a deposit or signing an agreement where possible. If you have already signed or paid, explain what has happened and the current stage of the purchase.

Costs of a purchase

The usual costs of a purchase are below. Rates can change, so confirm them before you sign.

  • Title deed fee. 2% of the declared price, paid separately by buyer and seller, and not less than the property's tax value. The registry also charges a fixed service fee.
  • Earthquake insurance. A compulsory DASK policy is required for flats and houses.
  • Sworn translator. Needed at the registry if a party does not speak Turkish.
  • Valuation report. Not required for an ordinary purchase. It is required for a purchase made to obtain Turkish citizenship. The rule has changed several times, so confirm it with the registry.

Fees are calculated on the declared price, and the authorities can charge the difference with a penalty if it is below the real price.

Residence permits and citizenship

Owning a home gives no automatic right to stay in Turkey beyond the period allowed for your nationality. A short-term residence permit can be granted to foreigners who own property, and the Ministry sets the type and value of property that qualifies. Some neighbourhoods are closed to new residence permit registrations. The list is revised from time to time, so check it with the provincial migration office before buying in order to live at a particular address.

Citizenship by investment is a separate route. It requires property worth at least USD 400,000 or the equivalent, a note on the title deed that the property will not be sold for three years, and a valuation report from a firm authorised by the Capital Markets Board (SPK). According to the land registry, co-owned shares do not qualify. The conditions are set by regulation and have changed before, so check them when you buy.

What to include in your first enquiry

Please tell us where the property is, whether you have selected a property, whether it is completed or off-plan, and whether you have already signed anything or paid money. You can also say whether you will be in Turkey or wish to act from abroad.

Do not send passports, identity documents or sensitive financial records in the initial message. We will explain which documents are needed for the particular matter.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a lawyer to buy property in Turkey?

The law does not require one. A lawyer can review the title deed record, the permits and the contract before you pay, and can act for you under a power of attorney. Without that review, problems such as a mortgage on the title deed, a missing occupancy permit or an invalid reservation paper may only come to light after payment.

Is the estate agent's or developer's lawyer enough?

A lawyer instructed by the agent or the developer acts for that client. A lawyer you instruct yourself acts for you. Ask who a lawyer is acting for, and instruct your own if you want the documents reviewed from the buyer's side.

Can you review a purchase before I pay a deposit?

Yes. We can review the proposed contract and the property documents before you pay or sign. Tell us the stage of the transaction and where the property is located.

Can you assist with a property in Alanya or Kemer?

Our office is in Antalya city. We handle property matters across Antalya province, including Alanya, Manavgat, Side and Kemer.

Can I arrange a purchase from abroad?

A purchase can be handled through a properly prepared power of attorney. The powers and documents needed depend on the transaction. Contact us before signing the power of attorney abroad.

Can I write to you in English?

Yes. You can write in English or Turkish by email, WhatsApp or the contact form.

How are your fees set?

Our fee depends on the work involved and is agreed before any work starts. See Fees and first contact. Tell us the stage of the purchase and where the property is, and we will explain what the work would involve.
